3. Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)

OLS fits an unpenalized linear hyperplane minimizing the training residual sum of squares via SVD-based least squares (numpy.linalg.lstsq) on standardized predictors \(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\) with an unscaled intercept column \(\mathbf{1}\).

$$\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^{p+1}} \|\mathbf{y} - \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 = \mathbf{e}^\top\mathbf{e}, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}} = [\mathbf{1},\, \tilde{\mathbf{X}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}, \quad k = p + 1 \tag{1}$$ $$(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}})\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top \mathbf{y} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top \mathbf{e} = \mathbf{0} \tag{2}$$

\(\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n\): training targets; \(\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{y} - \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\): observable sample residuals (distinct from unobservable disturbances \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\)). When \(\operatorname{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}) < k\), SVD returns the minimum-norm pseudoinverse solution \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}^{+}\mathbf{y}\).

Expand Matrix Derivation: Residual Maker, Hat Matrix & Gauss-Markov Assumptions
$$\mathbf{H} = \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}})^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathrm{aug}}^\top, \quad \mathbf{M} = \mathbf{I}_n - \mathbf{H}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}, \quad \mathbf{e} = \mathbf{M}\mathbf{y}$$ $$\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\mid \mathbf{X}] = \mathbf{0},\ \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\top \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_n \implies \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) = \sigma^2(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}, \quad \hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{\mathbf{e}^\top\mathbf{e}}{n - k}$$

Under homoskedastic, uncorrelated errors and full rank, OLS is BLUE (Gauss-Markov Theorem). Under heteroskedasticity \(\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^2,\dots,\sigma_n^2)\), classical standard errors require White’s sandwich estimator \((\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{e}\mathbf{e}^\top \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\).

4. Ridge Regression (\(\ell_2\) Regularization)

Ridge regression adds a squared \(\ell_2\) penalty on standardized slopes (leaving \(\beta_0\) unpenalized) to shrink variance and stabilize ill-conditioned \(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\). Ridge shrinks all coefficients smoothly toward zero and does not perform automatic feature elimination.

$$\min_{\beta_0 \in \mathbb{R},\, \boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \|\mathbf{y} - \beta_0\mathbf{1} - \tilde{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 + \alpha\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 \quad (\lambda \equiv \alpha \ge 0\text{ in }\texttt{scikit-learn}) \tag{3}$$ $$\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_\alpha = (\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{X}} + \alpha \mathbf{I}_p)^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{y}}, \qquad \hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y}, \qquad \kappa(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{X}} + \alpha\mathbf{I}_p) = \frac{d_{\max}^2 + \alpha}{d_{\min}^2 + \alpha} \tag{4}$$

\(\tilde{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{y} - \bar{y}\mathbf{1}\); \(d_1 \ge \dots \ge d_p \ge 0\) are singular values of \(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\). Note \(\texttt{sklearn.linear\_model.Ridge}\) omits the \(\frac{1}{2n}\) factor, matching Eq. (3) directly.

Expand SVD Shrinkage, Effective Degrees of Freedom & Bias-Variance Trade-off
$$\tilde{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{V}^\top \implies \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_\alpha = \sum_{d_j > 0} \mathbf{v}_j \frac{d_j}{d_j^2 + \alpha}\mathbf{u}_j^\top\tilde{\mathbf{y}}, \qquad \operatorname{df}(\alpha) = \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{R}^\alpha) = \sum_{j=1}^p \frac{d_j^2}{d_j^2 + \alpha}$$ $$\operatorname{Bias}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_\alpha) = -\sum_{j=1}^p \mathbf{v}_j \frac{\alpha}{d_j^2 + \alpha}\langle\mathbf{v}_j,\boldsymbol{\beta}\rangle, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_\alpha) = \sigma^2\sum_{j=1}^p \frac{d_j^2}{(d_j^2+\alpha)^2}\mathbf{v}_j\mathbf{v}_j^\top \preceq \sigma^2(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{X}})^{-1}$$

Unstandardized slopes are recovered via \(\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{unstd}, j} = \hat{\beta}_{\alpha, j} / s_j\) and \(\hat{\beta}_{0,\mathrm{unstd}} = \bar{y} - \sum_{j=1}^p \hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{unstd}, j}\bar{x}_j\). By Theobald’s theorem, \(\exists \alpha > 0\) with strictly lower parameter MSE than OLS.

5. Lasso Regression (\(\ell_1\) Regularization)

Lasso penalizes the \(\ell_1\) norm \(\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_1 = \sum_{j=1}^p |\beta_j|\), yielding a convex objective that sets weak or redundant predictors exactly to zero via soft-thresholding.

$$\min_{\beta_0 \in \mathbb{R},\, \boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \frac{1}{2n}\|\mathbf{y} - \beta_0\mathbf{1} - \tilde{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 + \alpha\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_1 \quad (\lambda \equiv \alpha \ge 0\text{ in }\texttt{scikit-learn}) \tag{5}$$ $$\hat{\beta}_j = \frac{1}{d_j}\mathcal{S}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j^\top \mathbf{r}^{(-j)},\, \alpha\right), \quad \mathcal{S}(z, \alpha) = \operatorname{sign}(z)\max(|z| - \alpha,\, 0), \quad \alpha_{\max} = \frac{1}{n}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{y}}\|_\infty \tag{6}$$

Here \(d_j = \frac{1}{n}\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j\|_2^2 = 1\) on standardized training columns, \(\mathbf{r}^{(-j)} = \tilde{\mathbf{y}} - \sum_{k \ne j}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_k \hat{\beta}_k\) is the partial residual, and \(\texttt{sklearn.linear\_model.Lasso}\) includes the \(\frac{1}{2n}\) scaling matching Eq. (5).

Expand Subdifferential KKT Optimality Conditions & Feature Selection
$$\frac{1}{n}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{y}} - \tilde{\mathbf{X}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) = \alpha \hat{\mathbf{s}}, \qquad \hat{s}_j \in \partial|\hat{\beta}_j| = \begin{cases} \{+1\}, & \hat{\beta}_j > 0 \\ [-1, +1], & \hat{\beta}_j = 0 \\ \{-1\}, & \hat{\beta}_j < 0 \end{cases} \tag{7}$$ $$\left|\frac{1}{n}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j^\top \hat{\mathbf{r}}\right| = \alpha \text{ for } \hat{\beta}_j \ne 0, \qquad \left|\frac{1}{n}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_j^\top \hat{\mathbf{r}}\right| \le \alpha \text{ for } \hat{\beta}_j = 0, \qquad \operatorname{df}(\alpha) = |A(\alpha)|$$

When predictors are highly correlated, multiple subsets can satisfy the KKT correlation bound \(\alpha\), explaining active-set selection variability across CV folds.

7. Methodology & References

Experimental Protocol

  • Persisted Splits: 80/20 splits (seed 42) are stored in data/processed/<dataset>/split.json so OLS, Ridge, and Lasso evaluate identical test observations.
  • Zero Leakage: Imputation, one-hot encoding, and standardization are fit only on training data and separately within each CV fold.
  • Penalty Parameter Mapping: Mathematical \(\lambda\) corresponds to scikit-learn’s alpha argument. Note that Ridge uses unnormalized SSE \(\|\mathbf{y}-\hat{\mathbf{y}}\|_2^2 + \alpha\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2\), whereas Lasso uses \(\frac{1}{2n}\|\mathbf{y}-\hat{\mathbf{y}}\|_2^2 + \alpha\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_1\).
  • Evaluation Metrics: \(\mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum(y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}\), \(\mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{n}\sum|y_i - \hat{y}_i|\), \(R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum(y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum(y_i - \bar{y})^2}\). For diabetes (LPM), Accuracy/Precision/Recall/F1 (0.50 cutoff), ROC AUC, and out-of-\([0,1]\) rates are reported.
