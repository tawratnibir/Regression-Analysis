  • On the held-out test split (n=301), the model achieves R²=0.5583 and RMSE=4.704 dB (compared to Train R²=0.5034, Train RMSE=4.827 dB).
  • Largest standardized predictor effects in absolute magnitude: frequency (-4.059), chord-length (-3.22), attack-angle (-2.367).
  • Exact OLS identities hold to machine precision: max |X_aug^T r|=3.07e-11, |mean(y) - mean(y_hat)|=2.84e-14, and predictions match scikit-learn LinearRegression within 5.68e-14.
  • Standardizing predictors changes the augmented design matrix condition number from 5.3103e+05 (unstandardized) to 3.525 (standardized), with rank 6 of 6.

7. Limitations and Potential Assumption Violations

  • Heteroskedasticity check rejects constant error variance (Breusch-Pagan p=9.99e-15), meaning classical homoskedastic OLS standard errors would be unreliable.
  • Training residuals deviate from Gaussian normality (Jarque-Bera p=3.95e-04, skewness=-0.008, excess kurtosis=0.559).
  • 90 training observation(s) exceed the Cook's distance 4/n threshold (max Cook's D=0.02143), indicating potential sensitivity to influential points.
  • Residual autocorrelation is indicated (Durbin-Watson=0.585, lag-1 corr=0.707), violating independent-error assumptions—especially notable for ordered/time-series data.
  • Linear models assume additive linear relationships between standardized predictors and the conditional target mean; nonlinear interactions require basis expansion or nonlinear models.
